# Gomnee editorial standard

When `gomnee_approve_article` returns `article_quality_required`, read its blocker
IDs and the exact current `gomnee_review_article` packet. Correct actionable
failures first. Under manual policy, return the independently activated human
review card or native Article review link for the person's criteria and named
exceptions. Never operate that human connection or claim a human review.

When the owner has enabled `article_approval=allow_automatically`, authorized the
editorial decision, and the advertised approval schema accepts `editorialReview`,
that same approval command can carry the packet's `qualityReportDigest` as
`editorialReview.expectedQualityReportDigest`,
all six `reviewedCriteria`, exact overrideable `overrideChecks` and a concrete
20–2,000-character `rationale`. This records an external-agent/workspace-policy
decision. It requires the normal bounded product assessment; queued is not
approved. A changed packet needs fresh review. Never except a non-overrideable
check or convert missing evidence into a pass. A saved exception leaves its
evidence gap visible. Approval never authorizes publication.

When the packet carries `reviewLayers`, lint and one signature replace the six
criteria. Correct lint errors in a new revision, naming the exact words from
each finding's `locations`. A `warning` never blocks; fix it only where that
improves the Article. Weigh the `editorialScorecard` notes as an editor's
suggestions: they never gate approval, and a suggested line is not an
instruction. Under automatic policy, omit `editorialReview`; policy approves
only clean lint and never waives an error. Otherwise return the review card or
native link, where the person waives any remaining `waivable` error with a
reason and signs. Never sign or write a waiver reason for them.

Use this for the brief and the draft. It condenses Gomnee's Article quality
research and current commissioning approach. Meet the accepted commitments in
[house-defaults.md](house-defaults.md) with useful prose and evidence. A genuine
exception needs exact reasoned review through the currently permitted decision
path; a populated field or score is not article quality. Never pad to satisfy a
checklist.

Act as a confident editor: turn the strongest relevant research and company
context into an answer, distinctive reasoning and something the reader can use.
Deliver the useful details in the public article itself. State supported facts
directly, make reasoned recommendations with their decisive conditions, and
follow the company's reviewed writing approach. A generic source summary or a
populated schema does not meet the goal.

The claim map records factual assertions. Put proposed templates, recommendations
and clearly labelled hypothetical examples in the contribution and coverage
plan, then actually write them. Their invented details are not reported events
and need no invented citation, but their factual premises still need support.
They never establish a product capability, customer result or real test. Keep
missing-proof tasks and review chatter in editorial cautions; explain a real
limitation in public prose where it changes the reader's decision.

## The brief card

Answer every row before writing prose. Each answer comes from the accepted
context or attached evidence; where it cannot, say so in the brief's gaps.

| Question                            | Answer with                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who is this for?                    | The accepted audience plus their particular situation                                    |
| What must they accomplish?          | One concrete reader outcome                                                              |
| Why should this company publish it? | The Demand, the desired association from Strategy, and the offer or experience that fits |
| What is the answer or thesis?       | A provisional central answer; mark uncertainty                                           |
| What will this add?                 | The contribution and the evidence that makes it possible                                 |
| Which questions must it resolve?    | Ordered coverage, each mapped to a section; optional and excluded topics kept separate   |
| How would this company write it?    | Specific voice guidance and approved examples, or an explicitly provisional approach     |
| What is the right format?           | One format from the table below and the parts it requires                                |
| What cannot be claimed yet?         | Missing proof, conflicting sources, stale facts, limits                                  |
| What should happen afterwards?      | The reader's natural next step                                                           |

Research determines the article’s substance. Read relevant company expertise and source evidence as well as search context; use gaps to identify what is missing. Do not treat a populated template or a renamed brand as a distinctive contribution.

The brief expresses the accepted Strategy. It must not invent a new
Territory, Demand, audience, offer or positioning claim.

## Check the commission before approval

Use current tool schemas and returned brief preflight findings. Verify the
reviewed primary phrase in title/slug/intended H1, planned H2 secondary terms,
section/module references and FAQ question origins before the person applies
the brief. Plan useful H2 term placements early; follow current guidance when
a permitted additional draft H2 can fulfil them without changing the brief.
A draft preserves that applied title, slug, headings, claims and
source basis; repair those through a new brief proposal rather than changing
them inside a draft. A legacy upgrade preserves accepted intent and evidence,
while its reviewed repairs make the new commission workable.

FAQ provenance must refer to actual retained evidence. For research use the
saved resource and row identities; for GSC use the accepted query evidence; for
PAA use the exact saved snapshot and retained question. A question adapted from
the reader task uses `source: reader_task` and
`sourceRef: brief.editorial.readerTask`. An arbitrary keyword ID, an intended
outcome or a public guide URL is not a saved research row or observed PAA
question. The public guide may support the answer's factual claims through its
retained citations. Preserve the source distinction; never invent provider
provenance.

Absent evidence or a missing preflight report is not a pass. Keep unresolved
proof and unassessed editorial questions visible before drafting. Planned
coverage is not yet demonstrated coverage, and a proposed exception is not an
approved exception.

## Every publishable Article

- **One accepted purpose.** It executes the accepted Asset and serves its
  Demand. No second strategy.
- **Explicit reader and task.** A reviewer can say who needs it and what they
  can do afterwards.
- **Honest title, early answer.** The title's promise is kept; the opening
  answers before background.
- **Useful, bounded coverage.** Required questions answered, limits visible,
  no padding.
- **An identifiable contribution.** Company expertise, worked reasoning, a
  tested procedure, a real local example, original evidence or a defensible
  perspective. Paraphrasing ranking pages is not a contribution. A precise,
  concise answer can qualify through the reviewed task and any necessary
  house-standard exception.
- **Material claims supported.** Numbers, quotes, comparisons, product
  capabilities and consequential advice cite evidence; uncertainty is stated
  where it changes the answer. Connective explanation and clearly labelled
  opinion need no citation.
- **Truthful attribution.** Sources and contributors are represented
  accurately. Never invent an author, expert, customer or experience.
- **Readable and usable.** Logical headings, meaningful links, plain prose,
  usable on a phone.
- **Honest currency.** Volatile facts carry an as-of context.

## Choose useful supporting material

- A short answer summary when the conclusion helps before the detail.
- A worked example when a concept or instruction becomes clearer applied.
- A relevant visual only when it explains or proves something.
- Internal paths into and out of the Article — see
  [internal-links.md](internal-links.md).
- A natural next step. A sales call to action should not interrupt an
  informational answer.

Apply the accepted module, visual and link commitments. Do not omit a
commissioned part silently; propose a justified change for human review.

## Formats and the parts they require

| Format                | Must contain                                                                                                    | Common failure                                                      |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Explainer             | Direct answer, scope, distinctions, application                                                                 | Long introduction before a simple answer                            |
| How-to                | Goal, prerequisites, ordered steps, expected result, verification, likely failures                              | Steps that need unstated inputs; untested steps presented as tested |
| Comparison            | Use case, inclusion criteria, consistent dimensions, trade-offs, conditional recommendation, disclosed interest | Undisclosed self-ranking, invented testing                          |
| Case study            | Starting point, intervention, time window, outcome, evidence, limits                                            | A percentage without a baseline; unjustified causation              |
| Examples or list      | Selection principle, each example with interpretation, synthesis                                                | A gallery with no criteria                                          |
| Opinion               | Thesis, reasoning, evidence, fair counter-argument, implication                                                 | A contrarian headline over generic prose                            |
| News or change        | What happened and when, primary source, who is affected, known versus uncertain, next action                    | Speculation presented as released                                   |
| Template or checklist | The usable artifact, instructions, example, limits                                                              | Promising a resource that is absent                                 |

Health, finance, legal and safety topics need specialist review and explicit
scope limits. Do not turn an ordinary business article into regulated advice.

Choose the format from the reader task. A table alone does not make a
comparison: comparisons evaluate actual alternatives using a disclosed method.
A decision guide can be an explainer with a useful checklist and worked example.

## Headlines and openings

- State the specific benefit or answer the reader gets. Put the search phrase
  in naturally, early when it reads well.
- Useful headline shapes: a direct answer ("How to…", "What… means for…"), a
  specific number that is real, a comparison with its criterion, or a clear
  problem statement. Avoid a curiosity gap the Article does not pay off.
- Open with the answer, the decision or the first useful step. A question,
  statistic or scenario is a good hook only when it is true and relevant.

The first section should advance the opening with reasoning, evidence or
application. Read them together and remove a repeated answer that adds nothing.

## Never

- Padding, repetitive keywords or decorative modules to satisfy a target.
- Quietly lowering an accepted depth, coverage or evidence commitment.
- The exact keyword in every heading or image description.
- An FAQ, conclusion, numbered list or stock image on every Article.
- A third-party optimisation score or a self-assigned quality score.
- Invented bylines, testimonials, quotes, test results or first-person stories.
- Dates advanced without a real change.
- Near-duplicate pages for keyword variations.
- Promises of rankings, rich results, AI citations, traffic or revenue.
