# Linking to the site's own pages

Internal links help a reader take the next step and help search engines
understand how the site's topics connect. They only work when they point to
real, relevant pages.

## Where to find link targets

- Pages and published Articles present in the Article context: owned sources,
  the site's library and earlier publications for the same Strategy.
- The site's public sitemap or navigation, when you can read them.
- When advertised, `gomnee_find_internal_links` ranks saved page metadata and can return fewer
  candidates or none when the inventory has no relevant pages. Its inbound
  suggestions preserve the exact writer-authored plan for current inventory
  pages; they are not newly generated insertion sentences or proof of a live
  destination. Read source passages and write a fitting transition before
  submitting the brief. Preserve the applied anchor sentences in the draft.
- Never invent a URL. If the right page does not exist, say so to the person
  — it may be a content gap worth a new Asset — instead of linking somewhere
  merely related.

## Which links to add

- **Into the task:** a prerequisite or definition page the reader may need
  first.
- **Out of the task:** the natural next step — a deeper guide, a related
  Demand's Article, or the relevant offer page when it fits the reader's
  intent.
- **Within the topic:** Articles serving the same Territory, so related pages
  reinforce each other.

The house default is 3–5 relevant contextual inventory links in their planned
sections and 2–3 inbound source-page suggestions with complete anchor sentences.
Choose useful pages from the actual inventory. Sparse inventory needs a
specific reviewed smaller plan and human exception; irrelevant links cannot
satisfy a target. Follow current findings and accepted commitments.

Inbound suggestions are the owner's separate insertion checklist. Proposing
them does not edit other pages or place inbound links automatically. Saved
inventory and acquisition dates are not fresh URL-resolution evidence.

## How to write them

- Descriptive anchor text that says what the reader gets ("how to check
  booking-page speed"), never "click here" or "read more".
- Place the link where the reader needs it, inside the sentence it supports.
- Link to the canonical address of the page, without tracking parameters.
- External links go to primary or authoritative sources and support a
  specific claim.
